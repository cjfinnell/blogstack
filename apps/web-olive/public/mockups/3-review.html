Safta says it's Mediterranean. Safta is being polite.

The short version

  • Go for the mezze. Six plates and a bottle, out for about $140 for two.
  • The gigantes are the best nine dollars on any menu in this city.
  • Skip the whole-fish special — priced like an occasion, cooked like a Tuesday.
  • The vegan legend is now printed on the menu. It was not, a year ago. That matters.
  • Ask for the counter. The dining room is beautiful and twice as loud.

I want to be fair to Safta, because the room is genuinely beautiful and because I have now eaten there three times and paid three times, which is more than most people who have opinions about it.

But I have to start with the menu, because the menu is where the problem lives. It says "Mediterranean." Mediterranean is twenty-two countries and roughly four thousand years, and using it as a cuisine descriptor is a way of not saying "Israeli," or "Lebanese," or "Greek," which are the three things this kitchen is actually cooking, sometimes on the same plate.

Naming the cuisine is not a political act. Refusing to name it is.

The gigantes arrive in a shallow bowl, the beans slumped and glossy, with enough dill that you smell it before it lands. This is the best thing on the menu and it is nine dollars, which tells you the kitchen knows exactly what it is good at even when the menu copy will not say so out loud.

The room has not been softened, and it should not be. Brick, glass, a wood oven you can see from every seat, and a volume that reaches its ceiling by eight and stays there. Sitting at the counter is not merely better; it is the only sensible choice — the tables along the wall get the same food ten degrees cooler and none of the reason you came.

What has changed since 2025, and changed for the better, is the labelling. A year ago the vegan options were recited by a server who was clearly improvising. Now they are printed, with a legend, and the kitchen will tell you which oil it fried in if you ask. That is a two-minute fix that most restaurants still have not made.

"We can do something for you" is not a menu. A legend is a menu.

What to order

Gigantes Must order

Butter beans braised soft in tomato and enough dill to be a decision rather than a garnish. Nine dollars. Order two bowls; you will not want to share the first.

Hummus, from the wood oven Must order

Served hot, which is the whole point, with a pita that arrives inflated and deflates on the table. Vegan as served — the labouring over "is there labneh in this" is over, it is printed now.

Charred cabbage, tahini, pistachio

A quarter head, blistered black at the edges and still sweet at the core. Fourteen dollars for a vegetable, and the only one on this list where that price feels earned.

Whole branzino Skip

Fifty-two dollars for a competent fish that has nothing to do with the rest of the meal. Give the money to the mezze and order another bottle.

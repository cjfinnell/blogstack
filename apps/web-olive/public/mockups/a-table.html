The kitchen at Tanuki finally stopped apologising

Tanuki Izakaya / 1412 Webster St, Oakland

The short version

  • Go for the grill. Six skewers, two beers, out for forty dollars.
  • The new tsukune is the best single thing we ate in Oakland this year.
  • Skip the sashimi board — it is priced like a destination and served like an afterthought.
  • Walk-ins at the counter before 6:15pm. After that, book a week out.

Eighteen months ago this room was trying to be four restaurants at once, and the menu read like an apology for not having chosen. There was a laminated section of rolls. There was, inexplicably, a burger. The charcoal grill — the reason the place had been worth writing about in the first place — was doing maybe a third of the covers, and the rest of the kitchen was hedging.

That version of Tanuki is gone. What replaced it is narrower, louder, and considerably better: a counter, a grill, forty seats, and a menu that fits on one card. The burger has been shown the door. The rolls went with it.

“The tsukune arrives with a raw yolk on the side and no instructions, which is its own kind of confidence.”

What remains is a kitchen that has decided what it is for. The binchotan runs hot from five o'clock and the skewers come in the order they finish rather than the order you asked for them, which is the correct way and still surprises people. Salt is used with an assurance that was not there in 2025. The chicken is broken down in-house; you can taste the difference in the neck and the tail, the two cuts that punish anyone buying pre-portioned.

The room has not been softened, and it should not be. It is a hard-surfaced rectangle that reaches full volume by eight and stays there. Sitting at the counter is not merely better, it is the only sensible choice — the tables along the wall get the same food ten degrees cooler and none of the reason you came.

What to order

Tsukune Must order

Chicken meatball, loosely packed, lacquered in tare and charred at the edges, with a raw yolk alongside. Order two per person. Nobody has ever regretted this.

Shishito, blistered

Ten to a plate, one in ten volcanic, finished with katsuobushi that moves in the heat. Three dollars. The best value on the card.

Negima Must order

Thigh and scallion, alternating, turned exactly once. The plainest thing here and the clearest evidence the grill cook knows what they are doing.

Sashimi board

Twenty-eight dollars for five competent slices of fish that have nothing to do with the rest of the meal. Give the money to the grill instead.

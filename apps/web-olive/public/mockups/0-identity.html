Pick one

The M

Pinyon has no swash alternate for its capitals, so there are two honest routes: keep Pinyon and draw the flourish separately, or move one step along the same engraved-copperplate family to a face that already carries a dramatic M. F1 is the closest to what you described.

F1 Monsieur La Doulaise Closest to the note
Maria Eleni

Same lineage as Pinyon, but the capital carries a long looping entry stroke as part of the letter itself, and the E answers it. Nothing is drawn on top, so it stays a real typeface — it can be set at any size, in any word, without redrawing anything.

F2 Miss Fajardose
Maria Eleni

Also has the flourished capital, drawn finer and set tighter. More delicate than F1 and a little more crowded between the two words. Worth seeing beside F1 rather than on its own.

F3 Pinyon Script, with a drawn flourish
Maria Eleni

Your original pick, untouched, with a flourish swept under the whole name rather than fused to the M. Running the swash into the M itself was tried and thrown away: at this size the join never quite lands, and it breaks differently at every screen width. This is the version of "keep Pinyon" that holds together.

Pick one

The bow

Rebuilt from B1 against your note. The ribbon is a constant-width band now rather than a tapering blade; the tails undulate instead of running straight out; the knot is a wrap with a seam across it and the loops darkening where they tuck underneath; and the surface is painted — a gradient across each band and uneven organic edges — rather than one flat colour. The only difference between these two is the paper grain.

W1 Painted

Gradient, soft uneven edges, visible knot. Smooth — reads closer to gouache than watercolour.

W2 Painted, with grain

The same drawing with paper texture through the wash, so the colour breaks up the way paint does on cotton paper. Closer to the photograph.

One thing to flag

About the reference photograph

We can't use that image itself — but we can have the thing you actually want

The bow in the photograph is printed artwork on a product from a stationery brand, and the wider shot has the maker's mark on it. Putting that painting on a public site would be using someone else's illustration as a logo, which is fine in a private notebook and not fine on a masthead.

The look is not the problem, though. A style — painted ribbon, periwinkle on cream, wavy tails, a visible knot — is not something anyone owns. Only that specific painting is. So:

  1. Paint one yourself and we scan it. Genuinely the best answer. It is an afternoon with a brush, it makes the mark actually yours, and it will have the wobble and the pooling that no amount of SVG trickery quite fakes. Paint three or four and we pick.
  2. Use the drawn version on this page. Original work, no licensing question, scales to any size, and no image to download so nothing slows the page down.
  3. Commission an illustrator for a painted original, if you want real paint texture without doing it yourself.

One and two are not exclusive either — a scanned painting on the homepage and the drawn one for the favicon and small sizes is a normal way to run this.

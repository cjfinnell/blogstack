Decision sheet · not a page design

The wordmark and the bow

Two things are worth settling before anything gets built for real, because they end up in the header of every page, the favicon, and every link preview — and they are painful to change afterwards.

Everything below is in the agreed palette. Nothing else about the site is being proposed here.

How to reply One script number and one bow letter — "S3 and B2" is a complete answer. If none of them are right, say which one is closest and what is wrong with it; that is more useful than picking the least bad.

Part one

The wordmark

"A little bit more twirly and a little bit more elegant, kind of like Blair Waldorf meets Elle Woods." These run in that order — S1 is the most Blair, formal and engraved; S6 is the most Elle, warm and exuberant. The current one is at the bottom for comparison.

S1 Maria Eleni Maria Eleni Pinyon Script Engraved-invitation formal. The most Blair Waldorf of the set — this is a wedding invitation, and it is elegant rather than playful. Least whimsical option here.
S2 Maria Eleni Maria Eleni Great Vibes Generous loops, confident downstrokes, still formal. The safe middle of the range — twirlier than S1 without tipping into cute.
S3 Maria Eleni Maria Eleni Allura Finer stroke, more air between letters, long twirling entry and exit strokes. The closest match to the ribbon in the photograph — same thin, painted line.
S4 Maria Eleni Maria Eleni Tangerine Calligraphic, very fine, dramatic swashes. Needs to be set large to work at all, which suits a centred masthead and nothing else. Weakest at small sizes.
S5 Maria Eleni Maria Eleni Style Script Bouncier and warmer, a signature rather than an invitation. Reads as friendly first, elegant second — the Elle Woods end, but still tidy.
S6 Maria Eleni Maria Eleni Yesteryear Retro, generous swashes, the most personality per letter. Fun, and the furthest from formal — worth seeing even if it is too much.
S0 Maria Eleni Maria Eleni Parisienne — current Rounded and casual. Legible and pleasant, but the letterforms sit upright and the loops are small, which is why it does not read as twirly.

Part two

The bow

Taken from the photograph: the ribbon is a band with visible width rather than a solid shape, the loops are open so the background shows through, the knot is small and tight, and the tails are long, uneven, and flick at the end. B1 through B3 are drawn as a stroked line, which is what keeps the loops open; B4 is the filled, tapered version for comparison. All four are one path each and scale to any size.

B1 Even ribbon Uniform band, open loops, two long uneven tails. The most direct reading of the photograph and the most legible when it shrinks.
B2 Wide and airy Thinner line, wider loops, longer tails that swing further out. The most delicate and the most whimsical. Loses definition first at very small sizes.
B3 Folded tails Tighter loops sitting higher, and tails that fold back on themselves the way a real ribbon does. More detail, more obviously hand-drawn.
B4 Painted, tapered Filled rather than stroked, with the band narrowing towards the knot to mimic a brush lifting. Closest to the paint texture in the photograph, heaviest on the page.

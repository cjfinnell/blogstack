The kitchen at Tanuki finally stopped apologising

Tanuki Izakaya · 1412 Webster St, Oakland · $$ · about $55 a head · directions

The cooking
Worth a detour.
Best yakitori within an hour's drive. We would cross the bridge for the tsukune alone, and have.
The room
Loud, hard, correct.
Counter only. The wall tables get the same food ten degrees cooler and none of the reason you came.

Eighteen months ago this room was trying to be four restaurants at once, and the menu read like an apology for not having chosen. There was a laminated section of rolls. There was, inexplicably, a burger. The charcoal grill — the reason the place had been worth writing about at all — was doing maybe a third of the covers.

That version of Tanuki is gone. What replaced it is narrower, louder, and considerably better.

The tsukune arrives with a raw yolk and no instructions, which is its own kind of confidence.

The binchotan runs hot from five and the skewers come in the order they finish rather than the order you asked for them, which is the correct way and still surprises people. The chicken is broken down in-house; you can taste it in the neck and the tail, the two cuts that punish anyone buying pre-portioned.

If you only order four things

Tsukune. Negima. Shishito. A second tsukune, because you will want it and the first one will already be gone.

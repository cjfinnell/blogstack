The kitchen at Tanuki finally stopped apologising

Tanuki Izakaya — 1412 Webster St, Oakland

The short version

  1. Go for the grill. Six skewers, two beers, out for forty dollars.
  2. The new tsukune is the best single thing we ate in Oakland this year.
  3. Skip the sashimi board — priced like a destination, served like an afterthought.
  4. Counter walk-ins before 6:15pm. After that, book a week out.

Eighteen months ago this room was trying to be four restaurants at once, and the menu read like an apology for not having chosen. There was a laminated section of rolls. There was, inexplicably, a burger. The charcoal grill — the reason the place had been worth writing about in the first place — was doing maybe a third of the covers, and the rest of the kitchen was hedging.

That version of Tanuki is gone. What replaced it is narrower, louder, and considerably better: a counter, a grill, forty seats, and a menu that fits on one card.

The tsukune arrives with a raw yolk and no instructions, which is its own kind of confidence.

The binchotan runs hot from five o'clock and the skewers come in the order they finish rather than the order you asked for them, which is the correct way and still surprises people. Salt is used with an assurance that was not there in 2025. The chicken is broken down in-house; you can taste it in the neck and the tail, the two cuts that punish anyone buying pre-portioned.

The room has not been softened, and it should not be. It is a hard-surfaced rectangle that reaches full volume by eight and stays there. Sitting at the counter is not merely better, it is the only sensible choice.

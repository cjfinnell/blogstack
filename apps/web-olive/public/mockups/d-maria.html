Inside a post

Read the whole thing →

I want to be fair to Safta, because the room is genuinely beautiful and because I have now eaten there three times and paid three times, which is more than most people who have opinions about it.

But I have to start with the menu, because the menu is where the problem lives. It says "Mediterranean." Mediterranean is twenty-two countries and roughly four thousand years, and using it as a cuisine descriptor is a way of not saying "Israeli," or "Lebanese," or "Greek," which are the three things this kitchen is actually cooking, sometimes on the same plate.

Naming the cuisine is not a political act. Refusing to name it is.

The gigantes arrive in a shallow bowl, the beans slumped and glossy, with enough dill that you smell it before it lands. This is the best thing on the menu and it is nine dollars, which tells you the kitchen knows exactly what it is good at even when the menu copy will not say so out loud.

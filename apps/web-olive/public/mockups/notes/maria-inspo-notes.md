# Maria's inspiration links — what each one actually does

All seven links from the 2:50 PM message, plus the sketch attachment. Fetched 3 Aug 2026.

---

## 1. chefchloe.com — flagged as the top inspo ("personality + simplicity")

- **Header:** centered "CHEF CHLOE" wordmark. Nav is almost nothing — one main link ("Chef Chloe Recipes"), social icons, email, cart. Hamburger on mobile.
- **Homepage flow:** banner image with a three-noun tagline ("VEGAN RECIPES. COOKBOOKS. COOKING CLASSES.") → restaurant location block with embedded map and ordering links → "About Chloe" with headshot → cooking-class carousel → cookbook card grid → social gallery → newsletter in footer.
- **Type/color:** sans throughout, neutral white/grey/black. Photography supplies all the color.
- **Recipes page:** single-column **vertical list**, not a grid. Card = landscape image on top, then byline, date, title, "Read More". **No filters, no categories, no search** — presented as a curated collection rather than a database.
- **Voice:** "No prior experience necessary", "LET'S COOK TOGETHER!"

**The lesson Maria is pointing at:** the personality is in the *copy and the photography*, not in the ornament. The chrome is nearly invisible. That is a very achievable target — and it argues against my brutalist Concept B.

## 2. charitymorgan.com

- Centered gold-on-black wordmark. Nav: Cookbook, Plegan Recipes, Chef's Tips, Contact. Social icons prominent.
- Homepage is **modular vertical scroll**: HBO special hero → cookbook CTA → extended bio → PLEGAN™ definition → three pillars (Creativity, Accessibility, Inclusivity) → testimonial → footer subscribe.
- **Palette: black + gold + rose/blush.** Elevated but warm. Large bold headlines, all-caps section titles, big size contrast to body.
- The `/plegan` page is the interesting one: an entire page devoted to defining a coined term and the philosophy behind it, sticky header, centered modular zones. **Philosophy given its own page rather than buried in About.**

**Lesson:** her "open letter to fellow foodies" deserves the Plegan treatment — a real page with design weight, not a paragraph on an About page.

## 3. zaccharybird.com

- Landing page is nearly bare: name, then the tagline **"VEGAN BUTCHER, BAKER & TROUBLEMAKER"**, two links (Recipes, Pop Up Events), social row.
- Nav taxonomy is **SAVOURY / SWEET / BUTCHER / BAKER** — categories named after the persona, not after content types.
- Recipes page: 3–4 column card grid, portrait/square images, **all-caps bold letterspaced titles**, sentence-case subtitles. No filters or sort on the page at all. Some cards link out to external publications (The Guardian, PETA, VegKit).
- Black on white, no accent color.

**Lesson:** the tagline does the entire personality job in seven words. Also: outbound cards (pieces published elsewhere) sit in the same grid as native content — useful if Maria writes for anyone else.

## 4. miyokos.com/blogs/blog

- Uniform card grid, landscape ~16:9 images.
- **Category tag sits above the title in the card's upper-left** ("Impact", "Brand") — same position Maria drew her tag chip, though she put it on the right.
- Bold sans headlines, white background, dark text, accent colors pulled from product photography.
- Sticky multi-level header with search and account. No visible pagination — load-more or infinite scroll.

**Lesson:** confirms the tag-on-card pattern from the sketch. The rest is a corporate brand blog and mostly not transferable.

## 5. cntraveler.com

- Nav: Inspiration, Destinations, Places to Stay, News & Advice, Shopping, CNT Triple Crown, Plan Your Trip. Drawer adds Cruise, Video, Women Who Travel, Search.
- Homepage is a long sequence of **named rails**: featured → Places to Go/Stay/Do → Trending → **Readers' Top-Saved Hotels** → video → featured destination → all destinations grid → gear → topic groupings.
- **Card anatomy:** category kicker in small caps colored text → bold sans headline → dek → byline. Images 4:3 or 1:1.
- **Hotel cards** are the relevant unit: name, **price tier $–$$$**, location, description, **award badge** (Readers' Choice, Gold List, Triple Crown), booking button.
- Sans headlines, serif body, black on white, navy accents, gold reserved for premium badges.
- Editorial devices: numbered galleries ("12 Best Family Beach Vacations"), award badges, bylines-as-authority, price tiers, "Listen" audio flags.
- Subscription CTAs in four places including a ticker banner. **This part is the anti-pattern for Maria** — she banned popups and ads.

**Lesson:** the award badge + price tier + numbered-list vocabulary is directly reusable for her values-driven awards idea.

## 6. jetsetchristina.com

- Wordmark with dark/light variants. Nav is huge: Book a Trip, Travel, About, Honeymoons, Trending Posts, Shop, Lifestyle — multi-level dropdowns with 50+ categories.
- Homepage: featured posts → **"Most Popular Must-Reads"** → lifestyle carousel → personal stories → newsletter ("Join the Jetsetters") → search + destination guide.
- Cards: large images at varied aspect ratios (1067×1600, 660×840, 880×1080 — portrait for people, landscape for scenery), 80–120 char headline, byline, date, comment count, truncated preview, "READ MORE".
- Sans, likely system fonts. First-person voice everywhere: "My perfect day involves a beach, a killer sunset, and great bottle of wine."
- Monetization is affiliate-heavy: Shop nav item, partner section in footer, advertiser policy.

**Lesson:** the first-person voice and "Most Popular Must-Reads" rail are worth stealing. The 50-item dropdown nav and the affiliate machinery are exactly what Maria's brief rules out.

## 7. rosewoodhotels.com/en/hong-kong

- **Hamburger left, centered wordmark** — same asymmetry as Maria's sketch, which has the hamburger on the right.
- Full-viewport video hero at 40% black overlay with one line of copy: "The soaring city estate."
- Sections: What's On (3 cards) → offers carousel → Culinary Experiences (3 restaurants) → Accommodation (4 tabs) → Experiences → Shop → gallery carousel.
- Sans throughout, sentence case body, **uppercase section headers**, tight letterspacing. Neutral blacks/whites/warm greys, brown 10% overlays on imagery.
- Cinematic 16:9 crops, very generous section padding, uppercase "RESERVE" buttons.
- Luxury devices: award callout ("No. 1 on The World's 50 Best Hotels 2025"), narrative copy ("Curated moments. Unforgettable stays"), restraint as the signal.

**Lesson:** restraint + uppercase letterspaced section labels + enormous whitespace is the luxury vocabulary. But the video hero and carousels are the "slow loading bullshit" she vetoed — take the typography and the spacing, not the motion.

## What the seven have in common

1. **Sans-serif, black-on-white, photography carries all the color.** Six of seven. Nobody is using a fancy display serif. My three earlier concepts all led with an expressive serif — that is a real divergence from her references.
2. **Tiny navigation.** Chef Chloe has one link. Zacchary Bird has two. Rosewood hides everything behind a hamburger. Only the two commercial publishers (CNT, Jetset Christina) have big nav — and both are ad/affiliate businesses. This validates her instinct to cut to Food | Wine | About.
3. **Personality lives in copy, not chrome.** "Vegan butcher, baker & troublemaker." "Let's cook together." "My perfect day involves a beach…" None of it is a design feature.
4. **Philosophy gets its own page.** Charity Morgan's /plegan is the model for the open letter.
5. **Category tag on the card** — Miyoko's puts it top-left over the image, Maria drew it right of the title, CNT uses a colored small-caps kicker above the headline. All the same idea.
6. **Almost none of them filter or sort.** Chef Chloe and Zacchary Bird both ship recipe indexes with zero filtering. Worth noting against my push for heavy faceting — for a small archive, a curated list beats a filter UI, and faceting can wait until the post count justifies it.
7. **Badges and price tiers** (CNT) are the vocabulary for her values-driven awards.

## Where the references conflict with her own brief

- CNT runs four subscription CTAs including a ticker; Jetset Christina is affiliate-funded throughout. She banned both. Take their *editorial* patterns, leave their business model.
- Rosewood's video hero and multiple carousels are the load-time problem she named. Take the spacing and the uppercase labels only.
- Miyoko's uses infinite scroll / load-more. Fine either way, but real pagination is better for URLs and crawling.

## Implication for Concept D

Concept D currently leads with Cormorant Garamond, a display serif. Her references say sans. Two options worth testing:

- **D-serif (current):** script wordmark + Cormorant display + Karla body. More "personal editorial", closer to the bow-and-script feeling of the sketch.
- **D-sans:** script wordmark for the logo only, then an all-sans page (Karla or Jost) with uppercase letterspaced section labels borrowed from Rosewood. Much closer to Chef Chloe, and the script mark carries all the personality by itself.

The script signature + bow already does the feminine/personal work. Setting everything else in a quiet sans may actually make the wordmark hit harder — the contrast is the design.

# Round 3 feedback — the identity, nearly closed

Maria reviewed the identity sheet and replied in one note, with a close-up
photograph of a painted bow attached.

---

## What she decided

- **The palette is approved.** "Yes to the colors." Locked, no further review.
- **The wordmark direction is Pinyon Script.** "I like S1 the best by far."
- **The bow is B1.** "B1 is closest."

That is the whole of the identity settled in principle. What came back were
three corrections and one question.

## The three corrections

1. **The M needs drama.** *"I want there to be more dramatic tails on the M
   leading up to it."* Pinyon Script has no swash alternate for its capitals,
   so this cannot be solved by turning on an OpenType feature. Two honest
   routes, both now on the sheet: move one step along the same engraved
   copperplate family to a face that already carries a flourished M, or keep
   Pinyon exactly and draw a flourish separately.

   Fusing a drawn swash into the M itself was tried and thrown away. At
   masthead size the join never quite lands, and because the swash is an
   absolutely positioned SVG and the M is a glyph, the two drift apart at
   different screen widths and different font-loading states. The version that
   holds together sweeps the flourish under the whole name instead.

2. **The tails need to be wavy.** The round 2 bow ran its tails out in a
   straight diagonal. They now undulate, with two inflections each and no
   left-right symmetry.

3. **It needs contrast, silk and a visible knot.** *"I want it to look silky
   and watercolor-y and you can see how the knot was tied."* Four changes, all
   read off her close-up:

   - The ribbon is a constant-width band rather than a tapering blade. Her
     photograph shows a ribbon of even width; round 2 drew a shape that
     narrowed to points, which is why it read as a bowtie rather than silk.
   - Each band carries a gradient rather than one flat fill, and the two sides
     of the bow use different gradients, so the loops read as being at
     different angles to the light.
   - The edges are displaced by a noise filter, so no outline is mechanically
     smooth.
   - The knot is drawn as an actual wrap: a rounded band over the centre, a
     seam curving across it, a highlight along its top edge, and the loops
     darkening where they disappear underneath.

   Two finishes are offered: with and without paper grain through the wash.

## The question, and the honest answer

She asked, of the close-up: *"this one is the perfect example if we can
basically just use that?"*

**We cannot use that image.** It is printed artwork on a product from a
stationery brand — the wider shot from round 2 has the maker's mark visible in
the corner. Using it as the site's mark would be putting someone else's
illustration on a masthead.

The style, though, is not the problem. Painted ribbon, periwinkle on cream,
wavy tails, a visible knot — none of that is ownable. Only that specific
painting is. So the sheet offers three routes, and recommends the first:

1. She paints one and we scan it. Best answer: it makes the mark genuinely
   hers, and real paint has pooling and wobble that SVG only approximates.
2. Use the drawn SVG on the sheet. Original, no licensing question, scales to
   any size, no image request.
3. Commission an illustrator.

One and two combine well — a scan for the homepage, the drawn version for the
favicon and small sizes.

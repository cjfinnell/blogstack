3 August 2026 · Oakland

The kitchen at Tanuki finally stopped apologising

Three visits, all unannounced. I paid every time.

Eighteen months ago this room was trying to be four restaurants at once, and the menu read like an apology for not having chosen. There was a laminated section of rolls. There was, inexplicably, a burger.

That version of Tanuki is gone. What replaced it is a counter, a charcoal grill, forty seats and a card you can read in one breath. The burger has been shown the door. The rolls went with it, and nobody at the counter on a Thursday in July seemed to be mourning either.

The tsukune arrives with a raw yolk and no instructions, which is its own kind of confidence.

The binchotan runs hot from five and the skewers come in the order they finish rather than the order you asked for them, which is the correct way and still surprises people. The chicken is broken down in-house; you can taste it in the neck and the tail, the two cuts that punish anyone buying pre-portioned.

Plainly

Go. Sit at the counter, before 6:15pm, or book a week out. Order the tsukune twice. Skip the sashimi board. About $55 a head with two drinks — 1412 Webster St, Oakland.
